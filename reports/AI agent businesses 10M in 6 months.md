# Abandon the $10M fantasy, chase power-law products

No documented business has reached $10M within 6 months with no outreach and AI agents doing all the work, and the evidence says none can be built on purpose in 2026. All three conditions fail on their own. Reaching **$10M ARR in 6 months is a tail event involving roughly 3–5 companies worldwide**, among them Lovable, Bolt.new and Base44. **Every one of them did heavy distribution**: viral launches, existing user bases, founder audiences or paid creators. **Today's best agents still need humans** for payments, legal review and defending against customers who try to manipulate them. The models that come closest share a pattern. They are software products whose output spreads itself, or that sit inside a store with built-in search, and agents run the operations while the founder still owns judgment and launches. For a Chennai CSE student with Azure, voice-agent and fintech/AML skills, the most realistic "unconventional" route is one of two things. The first is shipping many small, USD-priced, globally distributed AI tools, an approach that has paid off for solo builders but took 12–24 months and dozens of attempts. The second is a narrow vertical agent product in compliance or voice, where his domain knowledge is a moat. Expect $10K–$150K a month as a strong outcome, not $10M. Anyone selling the full fantasy matches the profile of schemes US and Indian regulators are now prosecuting.

## Only three or four companies have ever done $10M in six months

The documented $10M-in-6-months cases all come from one demand shock: vibe-coding tools in late 2024 and 2025. **Lovable went from $1M to $10M ARR in about 2 months** after its November 2024 relaunch ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/vibe-coding-platform-lovable-becomes-historys-fastest-growing-software-startup)). **Bolt.new reached about $20M ARR in roughly 2 months with 15 employees** ([Product School](https://productschool.com/resources/product-podcast/eric-simons-bolt-stackblitz-fastest-revenue-ramp)). **Base44, bootstrapped, hit $1M ARR three weeks after launch and sold to Wix for $80M about six months after founding** ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-base44-bootstrapped-startup-success-story-maor-shlomo)). Even Base44 had **8 employees at the sale** ([DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/wix-buys-ai-startup-base44-for-80m-six-month-sprint-deal)), so no true solo founder is documented at $10M in 6 months. The base rate shows how rare this is. **The top 100 AI companies on Stripe took a median of 24 months to reach a $5M annualized run rate** ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/at/blog/inside-the-growth-of-the-top-ai-companies-on-stripe)). Nearly all of these figures are self-reported run-rates rather than audited revenue.

These winners did not start from a blank slate. StackBlitz was a 7-year-old, VC-funded company with its own WebContainer technology ([Contrary](https://research.contrary.com/company/bolt)). Lovable grew out of the open-source GPT Engineer community ([Catalaize](https://catalaize.substack.com/p/lovables-path-from-open-source-to)). Shlomo was a repeat founder who built in public ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-base44-bootstrapped-startup-success-story-maor-shlomo)). Cal AI is the closest thing to a teenage outsider success, with a **$30M+ run rate and no outside funding**, but it got there in about a year, not six months ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/interviews/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-2025)), and it ran **a paid program with about 250 TikTok and Instagram creators** ([eChai](https://echai.ventures/how-founders-use-ai/r/cal-ai-case-study-0-to-50m-arr-in-18-months--2931)). "No outreach" in these stories means no outbound sales, not no marketing.

## Agents run tasks, not companies

The best real-world test of an AI-run business is Anthropic's Project Vend. In phase 2 it mostly eliminated loss-making weeks, but **it made $2,649 in a quarter against a $15,000 target**. Its CEO agent approved lenient requests about 8x as often as it denied them. It nearly signed an onion futures contract that US law bans, and humans still approved purchases and restocked the shelves ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2)). Benchmarks tell the same story. **Top models reach 12–16+ hour task horizons at 50% reliability but only about 1–3 hours at 80%** ([METR via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METR)). In Vending-Bench 2, the best model ends a simulated year with about $16K, while a good human strategy reaches about $63K ([Andon Labs](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2)). Klarna's reversal shows the cost of over-automating customer support: it rehired humans over quality problems ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/klarna-ceo-reverses-course-by-hiring-more-humans-not-ai/491396)). Polsia's claimed $4.5M–$10M run rate as an "AI that runs your company" is unverified and founder-reported ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/26/the-one-person-unicorn-myth-miracle-future-of-startups-polsia/)).

The practical design rule is to let agents handle high-volume, reversible, low-stakes work such as coding, QA, content and tier-1 triage. Put human gates on money movement, legal commitments and anything a customer could exploit. Base44's founder worked this way: AI did triage, QA and marketing content, and he **shut off his support bot after two weeks to stay close to customers** ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/18/solo-founders-ai-automation-entire-teams-entrepreneurs/)).

## The closest models are self-spreading products and store-native tools

| Model | Distribution mechanism | Credible solo outcome | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viral consumer AI app (shareable outputs) | Users share outputs | Photo AI: ~$100K+/mo after ~18 months | Spikes fade; Levels' game hit $1M/yr run rate in 17 days, then went to zero |
| Chrome extension / store-native tool | Store search, featured badges | 5-figure MRR within ~2 years | Poor conversion: 400K users produced $40K ARR |
| Programmatic SEO with real data | Google organic search | Self-reported $8K–$14K/mo | Scaled-content-abuse policy, AI Overviews |
| API / MCP / Apify actors | Marketplace listings | Apify paid $4M+ to all developers in total | Most MCP servers earn about zero |
| AI vertical SaaS in a hot category | Founder posting plus virality | Base44/Lovable-type tail | Needs category timing and launch skill |

Photo AI made about $5.4K MRR in its first week and was at roughly $132K MRR by 18 months, but it **came after about 70 failed projects** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/how-one-photo-ai-app-generates-132k-monthly-after-70-failed-startups/)). Levels' flight-sim game reached a $1M/year run rate in 17 days and then went to zero ([DEV](https://dev.to/promptway/he-built-a-flight-simulator-in-three-hours-and-hit-1m-a-year-in-17-days-then-it-went-to-zero-1b1l)). In store-native extensions, featured listings produced **5-figure MRR within two years** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/leveraging-a-marketplace-and-existing-user-bases-to-hit-a-5-figure-mrr-in-two-years-ltsw3AYvsvslFIYZtYZG)), yet another extension turned **400K users into just $40K ARR** ([First MRR](https://first-mrr.com/study/chrome-extension-unnamed)). Apify has paid more than **$4M to Actor developers in total** ([DEV](https://dev.to/nicolas_fainstein/the-state-of-mcp-monetization-in-2026-28i4)). Individual MCP server earnings are tiny, so marketplaces work as a channel, not as the business. Programmatic SEO and faceless content carry the most platform risk. Google's scaled-content-abuse policy ([Coalition Technologies](https://coalitiontechnologies.com/blog/google-march-2024-core-update-bad-news-for-low-quality-content)) and YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" monetization rule ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/youtube-ramps-up-campaign-against-ai-slop-with-stricter-monetization-caveats-on-inauthentic-and-repetitive-videos)) both target mass AI output, which is exactly what "agents do everything" produces.

## A Chennai founder's edge is cost arbitrage plus domain depth

The highest-yield setup for an Indian technical founder is to **price in USD for global buyers while living on INR costs**. Domestic SMB tools earn less per customer and compete with free government infrastructure. ONDC's own WhatsApp bot "Sahayak" already serves small sellers in several languages, built on Bhashini ([domain-b](https://www.domain-b.com/technology/information-technology/ondc-launches-whatsapp-bot-sahayak-to-provide-language-support-to-small-sellers)), and Gupshup runs a conversational ONDC buyer app ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/gupshup-rolls-out-indias-first-conversational-buyer-app-on-ondc)). Vernacular voice commerce is real, but large incumbents such as Meesho hold that ground.

The research notes do not cover AML or voice-agent markets directly, so what follows is a judgment call, not a sourced finding. The founder's rarest asset is fintech/AML knowledge combined with the ability to build voice agents, so the unconventional bet is a narrow, self-serve, developer-facing product rather than a consumer play. Examples are an AML or transaction-screening API, or a voice-agent testing harness sold on Azure Marketplace, Apify or MCP directories. Each would carry a public free tier and usage-based USD pricing. Agents would run the build, QA, docs and support triage, with the founder keeping compliance judgment, because a wrong AML decision is irreversible. This kind of product cannot reach $10M in six months. It can compound, it suits someone without an audience, and it can later be sold to a larger player, which is how Base44 ended up. One caution: regulated buyers usually want a human relationship, which works against the zero-outreach rule.

## The pitch you described is the regulators' favorite target

"AI agents do all the work, passive income, huge sums fast" is the exact pitch behind the FTC's Operation AI Comply. **Ascend Ecom and Ecommerce Empire Builders were permanently banned from selling business opportunities, with over $35M in alleged consumer harm**. Click Profit sold an "automated, AI-powered system," and the FTC sued Air AI over earnings claims in 2025 ([Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus/)). In India, **SEBI removed over 1.2 lakh misleading finfluencer posts** ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/sebi-takes-down-1-2-lakh-misleading-finfluencer-posts-steps-up-ai-surveillance-152481.html)) and **impounded Rs 546 crore** from an unregistered finfluencer ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/-79349.htm)). That matters for anyone tempted by AI trading bots or tip channels, the most common "unconventional" fintech idea. If a course, Telegram group or "system" promises this outcome, the seller's real business is selling the course.

## Conclusion

Asking for a business that makes $10M in 6 months with no outreach and full automation means removing the three things that actually produced the real outliers: timing a category shock, distributing aggressively, and keeping human judgment where mistakes are costly. The useful reframe is to treat the rare outlier as the right tail of many cheap attempts. AI agents mostly make each attempt cheaper; they do not make any single attempt likely to win. A founder who ships 10–20 small USD-priced tools a year, each with built-in sharing or store distribution, gets far more lottery tickets than one who looks for a single perfect autonomous machine.

For this founder, "unconventional" should mean an unusual combination of skills, not an unusual promise. Few solo builders can combine AML domain logic, voice agents and Azure deployment in one product, and that overlap is a better moat than any growth hack. The open question the research could not settle is how far self-serve distribution can go in compliance tooling. Test that early, with a free public tool, before committing a year to it.
